## Watering

The Boston fern prefers soil that stays evenly and consistently moist but never waterlogged. Water the Boston fern whenever the top half inch of the potting mix begins to feel dry to the touch. In a warm, well-lit room the Boston fern often needs watering two or three times per week, while in winter once every five to seven days is usually enough. Because the Boston fern resents dry air, mist its fronds or set the pot on a humidity tray to keep relative humidity near 50 to 70 percent. Allowing the Boston fern to dry out completely will cause the delicate leaflets to brown and drop.

## Light

The Boston fern grows best in bright, indirect light and tolerates moderate shade. Place the Boston fern near an east-facing or north-facing window where it receives filtered daylight rather than direct sun. Harsh midday sun will scorch the fronds of the Boston fern and bleach their green color. If the Boston fern is kept in deep shade, its growth slows and new fronds emerge thin and sparse. A spot that stays between 18 and 24 degrees Celsius suits the Boston fern well throughout the year.

Even so, any plant material can cause mild stomach upset if a cat or dog eats a large amount, so the Boston fern is best kept out of a pet's regular reach. If a pet shows unusual symptoms after contact with the Boston fern, contact a veterinarian or a poison-control line for guidance. ## Soil and repotting

The Boston fern thrives in a loose, peat-based or coir-based potting mix that drains freely while holding moisture. Choose a pot for the Boston fern that has drainage holes, since standing water rots its fine roots. Repot the Boston fern every one to two years in spring, moving it up by one pot size when the roots fill the container. When repotting the Boston fern, you can divide a large clump into two or three smaller plants to propagate it. A light monthly feeding with diluted balanced fertilizer during spring and summer keeps the Boston fern lush.

## Common problems

Browning frond tips on the Boston fern usually signal low humidity, underwatering, or excess fertilizer salts in the soil. Yellowing fronds across the Boston fern often point to overwatering or poor drainage at the roots. If the Boston fern sheds many leaflets indoors, the most common cause is dry air combined with inconsistent watering. Inspect the Boston fern for scale insects and spider mites, which appear when the air is too dry and warm. Trimming dead fronds at the base helps the Boston fern channel energy into healthy new growth.
